## SOC-010 — A travel comparison needs a consistent basis before it needs a verdict.

- **Format / language:** LINKEDIN TEXT / en
- **Source:** [Dhaka–Jeddah/Madinah Flights: Open-Jaw, Baggage & Quote Checks (2026)](https://uraltravel.co.uk/blog/dhaka-to-jeddah-madinah-open-jaw-flight-strategy-biman-saudia) — How to compare flight quotes on the same basis
- **Decision / evidence:** Explain like-for-like comparison without manufacturing a winning fare. (SF-03; USEFUL SECONDARY at the stated scope.)
- **Status / gate:** DRAFT — PRODUCTION / SOURCE CHECK; G-01
- **Funnel / KPI:** TRUST; Substantive professional replies; qualified reference visits
- **Visual direction:** Text-only. Optional diagram of comparison fields; no simulated quote or invented price.
- **Do not claim:** No measured savings, route-frequency or general conversion claim.

### LinkedIn copy

A travel comparison needs a consistent basis before it needs a verdict.

Two prices can look comparable while describing different trips: one passenger versus a family, cabin baggage versus checked bags, a roundtrip versus an incomplete one-way quote, or a fare with different change conditions.

For an Umrah itinerary, the surface journey matters too. Arriving in Jeddah and departing from Madinah may avoid backtracking, but the transfers still have to be priced and arranged.

The useful editorial contribution is to make those differences visible:
• same dates and passengers;
• the actual flight segments and bags;
• transfers and any extra overnight stay;
• cancellation and change conditions.

Without matched quotes, “always cheaper” is a claim the evidence cannot support. A comparison can still be valuable when its output is a better decision process rather than a winning price.

The flight-comparison guide shows the questions behind that method.
