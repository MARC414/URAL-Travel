## 0. Two things that change the order of work

### 0.1 The domain blocks most of this — decide it BEFORE building links

The site is at `https://ural-travel.pages.dev` — a free Cloudflare subdomain.

**Backlinks point at a specific address.** If you earn 20 links to `ural-travel.pages.dev` and then
move to a real domain, you face a problem:

- Every link still points at the old address. A redirect recovers most of the value, but not all of it.
- Many directories and profiles **won't let you edit the URL** after listing. Those links are stranded.
- Several legitimate outlets (tourism boards, press, business registries) **will not list a
  `*.pages.dev` subdomain at all** — it reads as a developer preview, not a business.

**Recommendation:** buy the domain first (`uraltravel.com`-style, ~$10–15/year). It is a one-time
decision that makes every link below land on the right target forever. If you would rather not,
that is a legitimate choice — just accept that a meaningful share of Tier 1 and most of Tier 3
below will be closed to you, and some earned value will be lost on the eventual move.

**If you do buy one later:** tell me, and I will find-and-replace every copy line in this kit. Do
not hand-edit 20 files.

### 0.2 Honest expectations — this is the slowest part of SEO

Links are the hardest, least controllable part of ranking. Set expectations accordingly:

- Most **free Bangladeshi business directories are low-value** — often no-follow, often unmoderated.
  They cost 10 minutes each and move the needle very little. Do them because they are cheap and they
  establish your entity (and they help AI answer engines recognise you), **not** because they will
  lift rankings.
- **The real wins are few and hard:** a genuine editor linking to your visa data, or a forum answer
  that becomes the reference. Those take months and cannot be automated.
- **Anything that offers "500 backlinks for $20" is a penalty risk, not a shortcut.** See §5.
- Success looks like **5–10 quality referring domains over 3–6 months**, not a spike.

## 3. Tier 2 — the link magnet (the real play)

**Your single best free-link opportunity does not exist yet.**

The playbook calls a "visa-free countries for Bangladeshi passport" pillar page your #1 link magnet
([07](07-backlinks-free.md) §3). **I checked the built site: it is not there.** You have a `/visa`
hub and six country pages (Dubai, Malaysia, Maldives, Nepal, Singapore, Thailand), each already
carrying precise, sourced visa detail — but no umbrella answer to the question people actually
search and cite.

That page is the asset most likely to earn links **without you asking anyone**, because:

- "Visa-free countries for a Bangladeshi passport" is a high-volume, evergreen, factual query.
- It is exactly what a journalist, forum poster or blogger cites and links to.
- You already hold the expertise — the six country pages are 80% of the raw material.
- It is updated yearly, which gives you a natural reason to re-promote it each year.

**Recommendation:** build it as a proper pillar page — a sortable list of countries with entry type
(visa-free / VOA / e-visa / sticker), maximum stay, and the source for each rule — with each row
linking to your existing country guide. Then promote it in the communities in §4.

This is on-site work, so I can actually do the building if you want it. **It is also the highest-value
thing on this list.** Everything in Tier 1 is preparation; this is the one that earns links.

## 4. Tier 3 — outreach, guest posts, communities

### 4.1 Communities (mostly no-follow, still worth it)

Google may not count these links, but they drive traffic and they are what AI answer engines read
and cite. **Participate genuinely — answer the question fully in the comment, link only as the
source.** Self-promotion gets you removed and achieves nothing.

- Bangladeshi travel Facebook groups (highest reach for your audience)
- `r/bangladesh`, `r/travel` — answer real visa/cost questions
- Quora — same discipline

### 4.2 Guest posts — vetted starting points

| Site | Fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `golocalbd.com` | Good | Business/travel niche, 1500–2000 words, 1–2 links allowed, no AI-generated content |
| `evbeing.com` | Decent | Travel; 1200+ words, one do-follow link, accepts Asia topics |
| `famecritics.com` | Decent | Travel; 800+ words, no purely promotional posts |
| bdnews24.com, The Daily Star, Prothom Alo | **High value, hard** | National outlets. Only realistic with a genuinely newsworthy data story — see §4.4 |

**Finding more yourself** — these searches surface live opportunities:

```

site:.bd "submit guest post"

```

### 4.3 Outreach email template

Short, specific, no flattery. Editors delete anything that looks like a mailshot.

> **Subject:** Bangladeshi passport visa data — free source for your [article/readers]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I run URAL Travel Intelligence, a bilingual travel resource for Bangladeshi travellers. We maintain
> up-to-date entry requirements for [destination] — visa type, maximum stay, and what Bangladeshi
> passport holders actually need to show at check-in.
>
> If it's useful for your [piece on X / readers], the [destination] page is here:
> https://ural-travel.pages.dev/visa/[slug]
>
> Happy to provide current numbers or a quote on Bangladeshi outbound travel if that would help.
>
> [Your name], URAL Travel Intelligence

**Why this works:** it offers a source, not a favour. Ask for the link only if they use the data.

### 4.4 The advanced play (later)

Publish one **original data story** you can defend — e.g. a year-on-year comparison of BDT costs for
the five most popular routes from Dhaka. Journalists link to data they cannot get elsewhere. This is
how a small site earns a national-outlet link without a relationship. Worth doing once the domain is
settled.

## 5. What to avoid — these can hurt you

- **Buying links**, link exchanges at scale, PBNs, automated directory blasts, comment/forum spam,
  hidden links, irrelevant links. Google's link-spam systems devalue or penalise these.
- **Mass-submitting to hundreds of directories.** The 6–9 in §2 are cheap and fine. Fifty is a
  footprint that looks like spam and does nothing for you.
- **Paying for a "guest post" that is really a paid link.** Some sites in the search results above
  charge. That is a paid link by another name — skip it.
- **AI-generated guest posts.** `golocalbd.com` explicitly rejects them, and editors detect them.
  If I draft something for you, treat it as a draft **you rewrite in your own voice**.
