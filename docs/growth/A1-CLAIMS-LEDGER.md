## Ledger — shared claims M1–M5

### M1 — Travel quota: $18,000 / $5,000 cash / $9,000 minors — ✅ VERIFIED (P1)
- Exact circular wording: entitlement enhanced "to USD 18,000 or equivalent from USD 12,000";
  minors below 12 "shall remain at 50 percent"; "release of foreign exchange in the form of
  USD notes shall not exceed USD 5,000 (five thousand) per person within the applicable entitlement."
- Applies to: adult Bangladeshi nationals **residing in Bangladesh**, per calendar year,
  released by ADs for travel abroad. it is a ceiling on release, not an automatic grant.
- **Safe wording:** "Since 6 September 2026, Bangladesh Bank allows authorized dealer banks to
  release up to USD 18,000 per calendar year for an adult resident's travel abroad (minors under
  12: 50%, i.e. up to USD 9,000). At most USD 5,000 of that may be released as US-dollar notes;
  the rest is non-cash (e.g. international cards). It is a ceiling — your bank releases against
  your travel documents, not automatically. [P1]"
- Fixes required: articles cite "FE/PD-1 Circular No. 33" — correct formal cite is
  **FEPD-1 Circular No. 33 (6 September 2026)**. RFCD article's "(raised from $12,000 under FE
  Circular No. 33)" is right in substance; standardise citation everywhere. Calendar-year basis
  (Jan 1–Dec 31) comes from FE Circular 37/2025/GFET — cite P1 + "other instructions unchanged".

### M2 — The $300 online single-transaction limit — ⚠️ CONFLICT → QUALIFIED (P3, P2)
- The articles frame $300 as *banks' default switch setting*. **That understates it:** GFET
  Ch. 19 ¶17 sets USD 300 as the regulatory single-transaction limit for online purchases of
  goods/services against travel quota. **But** travel-specific uses sit in separate paragraphs
  (visa fees ¶14, online hotel booking ¶16), and banks in practice allow higher bona-fide travel
  payments against documents — the articles' "call your bank to raise it for a ticket/hotel"
  advice describes that practice, not a right.
- RFCD cards are different by explicit circular: online payment "at actual" (P2, 2024).
  The RFCD article's "no $300 cap" claim is therefore **VERIFIED for RFCD online payments**,
  with the P2 citation — but must not be extended to POS/ATM or to travel-quota cards.
- **Safe wording:** "Bangladesh Bank's card rules cap a single online purchase at USD 300 under
  the general online-payment paragraph, which is why large flight/hotel checkouts are often
  declined on travel-quota cards. In practice, banks may process a larger documented travel
  payment — ask your bank *before* paying, with ticket/hotel proof; approval is the bank's
  decision. Cards issued against an RFCD account are expressly allowed online payment at the
  actual amount (Bangladesh Bank, 9 May 2024). [P3][P2]"
- Fixes: remove "5-minute fix"/"banks immediately lift the cap once verified" guarantees;
  remove unsourced MCC code ranges or cite a bank page; keep bank-hotline advice as practice.

### M3 — RFCD deposits, FMJ, 30 days — ✅ VERIFIED with one article correction (P4, P5, P2)
- VERIFIED: open at an AD bank with foreign exchange brought in on return; ≤ $10,000 without
  declaration; any amount with FMJ; **no inward remittance** (article omission — add);
  balances freely transferable abroad; international card available; ≤2 supplementary cards (P5).
- **Article error to fix:** "open within 30 days of arrival" → the 30-day deadline applies only
  to depositing **FMJ amounts over $10,000** (P4). ≤ $10,000 may be deposited against previous
  trips (P4: "you have the option to deposit here against your previous trips abroad").
- **Safe wording:** "You can deposit up to USD 10,000 (or equivalent) brought back from a trip
  without a customs declaration. Larger amounts need the FMJ form completed on arrival, and
  that declared amount must be deposited within 30 days. An RFCD account cannot receive
  remittances or income from abroad — only foreign exchange you physically brought in. [P4]"

### M4 — DCC percentages — ❌ DROP the precision (no primary source exists)
- No regulator or network publishes a fixed DCC markup; it is set per merchant/acquirer.
  Articles currently state 4–6%, 4.5–6.5% and "5% trap" — mutually inconsistent.
- **Safe wording:** "If you choose BDT (or 'home currency'), the merchant's bank converts at
  its own rate, usually with a markup of several percent above the network rate. Choosing the
  local currency (SAR, THB, MYR, AED, SGD) makes your own bank/network do the conversion.
  The exact markup varies by terminal — the rule is to choose local currency, not a number."
- Also DROP: "losing roughly 2.5% to 3.5%" double-conversion figure in the cash guide
  (rewrite as spread explanation), and unsourced per-destination cash amounts
  (SAR 1,000–1,500 etc.) become labelled planning suggestions, not rules.

### M5 — Money-changer endorsement fee BDT 300 — ⚠️ QUALIFIED (S1; primary PDF pending)
- Corroborated by BSS + Daily Star + New Age (all 7 Jan 2026): max BDT 300 per passport
  regardless of amount; fee must be displayed; written receipt; records kept; fee is the
  **money changer's**, for selling notes/TCs under GFET-2018 — not a bank endorsement fee.
- Article currently states this correctly in substance ("Bangladesh Bank circular, 7 January
  2026"). **Fix:** separate it clearly from bank charges (article already does — keep), and at
  S3 either link the BB circular PDF if located or cite it as "Bangladesh Bank circular,
  7 January 2026 (fee cap for licensed money changers)".
